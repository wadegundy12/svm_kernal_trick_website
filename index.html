01 / The velvet rope

A linear SVM separates classes with a line. Its preferred line leaves the widest possible gap to the nearest points on either side. That gap is the margin.

01 — THE BOUNDARY

A line with a job.

In 2D it is a line; in higher dimensions it is a hyperplane. The two sides predict different classes.

02 — THE MARGIN

The widest safe corridor.

For separable data, a hard-margin SVM maximizes the distance to the nearest training points. Wider is better, subject to separating every point.

03 — SUPPORT VECTORS

The VIPs calling the shots.

Points touching the optimized margin pin the boundary in place. Move distant points without crossing the margin and the optimum stays put.

03 / The mathematical sleight of hand

Skip the elevator.
Keep the view.

Explicitly constructing thousands of features can be expensive. The SVM only needs dot products between transformed points. A kernel computes those directly.

K(a, b) = φ(a) · φ(b)

The kernel returns a number,
not a transformed point.

φ is a feature map. K is a shortcut to the dot product after that map. The SVM can train and predict using these numbers without ever building the feature vectors.

For this small example, φ(x, y) = (x², √2xy, y²). Its dot product is exactly the polynomial kernel K(a, b) = (a · b)².

That is the kernel trick: the same result, with the transformed coordinates left implicit. Some kernels, including the RBF kernel, correspond to infinitely many features.

A valid SVM kernel must produce positive semidefinite Gram matrices. An arbitrary “similarity score” is not automatically a valid kernel.

Choose your play.

γ: higher values make similarity more local; boundaries can become small islands.

C: higher values penalize margin violations more. Lower values tolerate more violations in exchange for stronger regularization.

THE RBF KERNEL

Nearby points look alike.

K(a,b) = exp(−γ‖a−b‖²). Close points have similarity near 1; distant points approach 0.

SOFT MARGINS

A little imperfection is allowed.

Real data can overlap. A soft-margin SVM balances a wide margin against points inside the margin or on the wrong side. Those can also be support vectors.

DON'T BET ON TRAINING ACCURACY

A perfect fit can fool you.

The percentage above uses training points only. Choose C and γ with validation data; fitting every observed point does not guarantee good future predictions.

f(x) = Σ αᵢ yᵢ K(xᵢ, x) + b

Predict by the sign of f(x). Only support vectors have nonzero αᵢ and contribute to this sum.
The margin is measured in feature space; its edges can look curved in input space.
